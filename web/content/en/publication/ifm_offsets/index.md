abstract: Forest carbon offsets are pivotal to the global climate strategy, yet their claimed benefits are often undermined by flawed baselines. This study assesses five Improved Forest Management projects in China by constructing ex-post counterfactual baselines from satellite observations from 2001 to 2020. Using synthetic control, statistical matching, random forest, and XGBoost methods, the analysis finds that reported static baselines systematically underestimate natural carbon stock accumulation, leading to over-crediting and weak evidence of additionality across the studied projects.
